PLAN YOUR CAMP

Frequently asked questions.

Everything you need to plan your GD1 camp.

01What do GD1 camps focus on?

GD1 camps focus on power skating and individual player development. Training develops edge control, balance, acceleration, skating mechanics, and efficient movement on the ice.

02Who coaches at GD1 camps?

GD1 is led by founder and head coach Dmitriy Gnitko, a former professional hockey player and graduate of the Druzhba-78 hockey school in Kharkiv.

View Dmitriy’s coach profile

03Is a GD1 camp suitable for my child?

The GD1 programme welcomes players of all ages and skating abilities. Training is adapted to each player’s experience, individual needs, and development goals, so everyone can work on the skills that matter most to their progress.

04How many training sessions are there each day?

Our current camps offer two on-ice and two off-ice training sessions daily. Check the individual camp page for its programme and any specific arrangements.

05How do I register for a camp?

Open the camp page and select Register now to complete the registration form. After submitting it, you’ll receive the prepayment details. Registration is confirmed once the required prepayment has been paid.

06How much does a camp cost?

Prices vary by camp. Each camp page lists the training fee and any available extras, such as accommodation or lunch. Check those details before registering.

07Is accommodation included?

Accommodation is separate from the training fee. Arrangements vary by location: some camps offer hotel accommodation, while others can arrange it upon request. The individual camp page explains the available options and prices.

08Are meals included?

Meal arrangements depend on the camp and the package selected. For example, Elektrenai offers a camp fee with lunch, while Prague offers meals by prior request. Check your camp page for the applicable arrangements and costs.

09What is the cancellation policy?

Current camps require a €100 prepayment per session. This is non-refundable if you cancel less than one month before the camp starts. If your plans change, contact GD1 as soon as possible to discuss your booking.

10What should players bring to camp?

Bring a complete set of hockey equipment for on-ice sessions, sportswear for off-ice training, and everyday essentials.

On-ice equipment

  • Skates, sharpened and ready for training
  • Hockey stick and a spare if available
  • Helmet with appropriate face protection
  • Full protective equipment, including neck protection
  • Hockey gloves
  • Training jerseys, hockey socks, and base layers
  • Skate guards and a towel for drying skate blades
  • Goalies: a complete set of goalie equipment

Off-ice training

  • Trainers suitable for exercise
  • Sports shorts or training trousers
  • T-shirts and a tracksuit or hoodie
  • Spare socks and a change of training clothes

Daily essentials

  • Refillable water bottle
  • Towel and toiletries
  • Any personal medication, with clear instructions
  • Weather-appropriate clothing

For overnight stays, also bring travel documents and enough everyday clothing for the trip. Please contact GD1 before departure to confirm any requirements specific to your camp.
